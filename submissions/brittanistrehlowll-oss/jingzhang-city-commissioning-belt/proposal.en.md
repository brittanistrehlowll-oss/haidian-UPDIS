# JING-ZHANG CITY COMMISSIONING BELT
## A CIVIC AI OVERLAY FOR THE AI-NATIVE CITY
### AI-era regulatory planning and real-world urban innovation

**One-sentence proposal.** This is not about giving AI a parcel of land or turning the city into a technology showroom. It is about making AI in a real city **expressible by planning, testable in real scenes, understandable to the public, overridable by humans, and removable when necessary**. Urban design and everyday life are the main body; the **Civic AI Overlay** is the planning innovation; **Compute–Power–Water** is the focused infrastructure support; C0-C6 is an operating method.

All spatial moves are conceptual open-call proposals. The package uses the repository-maintained `provisional_rough` geometry, not an official redline. Exact official polygons, statutory controls, ownership, heritage controls and engineering data must trigger recalculation and professional confirmation. ### No Standard Answer for a City: Human and Silicon Intelligence for Better Life

There is no single standard answer for a life, and even less for a city. The meaning of a city is not to be “solved” once by a planner, an institution, or an algorithm, but to enable the people who live there to keep sensing, choosing, creating, and revising. Cities have historically embodied and amplified human experience, institutions, and engineering intelligence. In the future, AI systems will increasingly contribute sensing, computation, reasoning, and coordination. We do not frame these two forms of intelligence as competitors, nor do we cast AI as a “super-brain” that replaces residents' value judgments. **Human intelligence and silicon intelligence share a common aspiration: to make our lives better.** Human intelligence contributes values, lived experience, ethics, emotion, responsibility, and final judgment; silicon intelligence contributes large-scale sensing, complex simulation, real-time coordination, and continuous learning. Their common purpose is not technology itself, but better everyday life, public value, and long-term urban resilience.

**The Civic AI Overlay is the planning layer; C0-C6 is only the operating layer.** Planning must first answer where an AI capability sits, what urban resource it can call, what permission it has, who is responsible, and how it degrades or exits. The gates then govern how that capability moves from simulation to bounded real-world use. Every AI proposal begins as a hypothesis and must pass rule and public-interest review, shadow operation, limited real load, and human-supervised operation. If evidence is insufficient, it stops; if risks cannot be controlled, it rolls back; if public value is not established, it should not proceed. **Intelligence may increase, but the boundaries of authority must remain explicit; systems may become smarter, but the city must retain human choice.** [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK] [depth:risk_missing_data]

Three professional principles structure the proposal. First, **AI creates new regulatory-planning objects**: edge-compute interfaces, flexible-energy interfaces, robot/logistics interfaces, sensing boundaries, operating permissions, human takeover and exit conditions cannot remain operational footnotes. Second, **AI infrastructure in the central city needs spatial matching, not an automatic assumption of new large data centers**; the focused planning questions are what compute belongs locally, when electrical load is protected/flexible/migratable, and what cooling-water conditions are acceptable. Third, **public interest must become spatially testable** through AI, human, offline, and low/no-sensing paths. This produces a clear hierarchy: urban design main body + Civic AI Overlay + C/P/W support + C0-C6 operating method. [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK] [depth:existing_conditions_diagnosis]

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

### Urban-design main body + Civic AI Overlay + AI infrastructure support + commissioning method

**Layer 1 | Urban-design main body.** The Jing-Zhang heritage park forms a continuous civic spine, while the three key areas carry different forms of urban reality: Zhongzhiyuan tests how AI R&D and products reach real-city interfaces; the AI Origin Community tests long-term everyday use; Dazhongsi tests how AI-era activities enter complex existing fabric through renewal and implementable planning rules. Public space, walking/cycling, active ground floors, community life and incremental renewal must work before AI is added. [standard:MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN-MEASURES]

**Layer 2 | Civic AI Overlay: the proposal's first planning innovation.** It is neither a new AI land-use class nor a substitute for statutory planning. It adds AI-era planning objects so **space - interface - permission - responsibility - evidence - exit** can be read consistently by planners, operators and machines.

**Minimum rule unit:**  
`Rule ID → Spatial Scope → Object → Requirement/KPI → Source → Responsible Party → Permission → Commissioning Gate → Evidence → Version → Re-test Trigger → Rollback/Exit`

Key objects include edge-compute interfaces, flexible-energy interfaces, sensing scope, robot/logistics and curb interfaces, public-AI access, automation permissions, human takeover, low/no-sensing routes, maintainable isolation and re-test/exit triggers. Statutory land use, FAR, height, road controls and legal procedures remain with formal planning and approvals.

The competition's GeoJSON, metrics, compliance, sources and version requirements are treated as a prototype argument: **future regulatory planning should be readable by both people and machines**. Humans read spatial intent, public value and implementation logic; machines read objects, boundaries, rules, status and triggers. Both must point to the same planning fact.

**Layer 3 | AI Infrastructure Support: Compute–Power–Water.** Infrastructure no longer carries a six-network concept. It focuses on three AI-specific spatial-fit questions:

|Topic|Planning question|Rule direction|
|---|---|---|
|**C | Compute Matching**|Which workloads need what class of compute, and which compute belongs in Jing-Zhang?|Regional compute handles large training/high-intensity tasks; district compute serves inference, digital twins, R&D and shared enterprise access; edge nodes serve low-latency, privacy-sensitive and high-reliability tasks. No default large AIDC in the core city.|
|**P | Compute–Power Coordination**|Not only “how much power?”, but what must run now, what can shift in time, and what can migrate?|Protected / Flexible / Migratable workloads coordinated with storage, flexible building demand and regional task migration.|
|**W | Compute–Water Coordination**|Are cooling mode, water source and peak-water conditions appropriate for dense urban fabric?|W0 edge nodes add no material cooling-water demand; W1 small/medium compute prioritizes low-water/closed-loop approaches; W2 dense compute triggers joint energy-water-heat review; W3 high-energy/high-water workloads should normally use regional supply.|

Communications, underground utilities, transport and logistics remain essential **connection, engineering and scenario-fit conditions**, but do not compete with C/P/W as an originality axis. No unverified MW, MVA, m3/day, pipe diameters or engineering alignments are invented.

**Layer 4 | C0-C6 + City Evidence Ledger: an operating method, not the first originality claim.** The gates move an already planning-defined capability from evidence baseline and simulation through rule review, shadow/dummy load, limited real load, human-supervised use, and recertification/rollback/exit. The ledger records Project ID, scope, responsibility, data/model version, risk, permission, test evidence, human intervention and exit state.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The overall design treats physical infrastructure, public space and governance rules as one system. A structured design model covers the site with research, park/green, commercial-service and community-service land-use classes. It is a machine-readable design model, not an approved statutory land-use plan. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001] [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE]

The **Civic AI Overlay is the proposal’s primary innovation**. It treats AI-era spatial interfaces and operating rights as regulatory-planning objects rather than another city OS. It uses three rule classes: A Mandatory for safety, privacy, public interest, human override, audit and emergency; B Conditional for equivalent-performance alternatives; C Guidance/Experimental for pilots. Each rule has scope, object, requirement/KPI, source, responsible party, approval gate, version, evidence, re-test trigger and rollback/exit.

Renewal follows diagnosis - deliberation - planning - action - commissioning - recertification. The current package uses only four conceptual building actions: retain/adapt, renovate/open ground floor, new testbed, and temporary/reversible use. It does not identify real buildings for demolition without ownership, structural, heritage and statutory-control evidence. ## Detailed Design of Key Areas

![Three key areas: testing, living and renewal](assets/figures/key-areas.en.png)

**Zhongzhiyuan - CITY TESTBED.** This is not a closed laboratory park or a collection of showrooms. It is the **last mile before an AI product reaches the real city**. A risk gradient links a Public Front, Controlled Test Core, Prototype Transition and Limited Real-Life Edge, with PUBLIC / TEST / SERVICE flows physically separated. Spaces include developer commons, a simulated city street, autonomous-system test yards, municipal-AI shadow operation, failure/repair testing and plug-in bays. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001]

C/P/W stays supporting: district and edge compute serve R&D/inference; storage, load simulation and emergency switching support compute-power testing; dense equipment only tests cooling/water constraints and does not imply a large data center. Passing a test creates evidence, not automatic operating permission.

**Beijing AI Origin Community - LIVING LOAD.** This is not a “future community full of smart devices” but a real-life load for testing whether AI can support ordinary life over time. The spatial main body is an everyday-life loop, active innovation ground floors, a community commons, universal-access walking and low-sensing quiet space. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002] [standard:BARRIER-FREE-ENVIRONMENT-LAW]

Basic public service uses four equivalent paths: **AI PATH, HUMAN PATH, OFFLINE PATH, and LOW / NO-SENSING PATH**. The community uses only lightweight edge compute justified by latency, privacy or reliability; high-intensity training is not introduced into residential fabric. AI is a capability residents can choose, not a prerequisite for life.

**Dazhongsi - RENEWAL RULE LAB.** Dazhongsi is not an AI shopping complex. It is the proposal's most planning-intensive **AI renewal-unit regulatory-planning workbench**, testing how stakeholder needs, public value, district-parcel constraints and implementation status become traceable planning rules. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-003] [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design]

Spatial actions are **KEEP / ADAPT / TEMPORARY / TRIGGERED**, avoiding premature demolition judgments where ownership, structure, heritage and statutory controls are missing. The workflow is **existing evidence → multi-party deliberation → spatial variables → public-interest check → economic/implementation feasibility → A/B/C rules → project actions → one-map maintenance → feedback and re-test**.

A Renewal Commons hosts residents, merchants, owners, government, operators and professionals; a district-parcel optimizer generates comparable options only; a rule ledger records provenance, responsibility, version and triggers. Priorities are active ground floors, walkable commercial continuity, community-service repair, adaptive reuse, temporary innovation and shared logistics. Lightweight edge compute in existing buildings is conditional on fire safety, power, heat rejection and maintenance; new load or cooling demand triggers Power / Water review.

AI may assist analysis and comparison, but cannot automatically decide FAR, property rights, compensation, demolition, statutory participation or approval.

### AI Infrastructure Support | Compute–Power–Water

**C | Compute Matching.** Use a three-level system: regional compute for large training/high-intensity work; district compute services for inference, digital twins, R&D and shared enterprise access; edge micro-nodes for low-latency, privacy-sensitive and high-reliability tasks. The planning question is workload-to-space matching, not “where to build a large data center”.

**P | Compute–Power Coordination.** Classify workloads as **Protected / Flexible / Migratable**. Public safety, emergency and critical public services are protected; ordinary inference, charging and some enterprise workloads can shift; training/batch workloads can migrate to regional resources. Planning therefore coordinates workload, time, grid state, storage and flexible building demand without claiming unverified available capacity.

**W | Compute–Water Coordination.** Apply W0-W3: W0 edge nodes create no material new cooling-water demand; W1 small/medium compute prioritizes low-water or closed-loop approaches; W2 dense compute requires joint energy-water-heat/heat-reuse review; W3 workloads imposing material energy/water pressure on the central city should normally be supplied regionally. Drawings show cooling mode, water-source conditions and Peak Water Review triggers, not invented m3/day values.

**Roles by place.** Zhongzhiyuan focuses on reliable compute interfaces, test compute, compute-power coordination and robot charging; AI Origin on lightweight edge inference, public service, low latency, low intrusion and emergency power; Dazhongsi on existing-building fit, low-cost shared compute and incremental-capacity triggers; the public spine receives only small micro-nodes, never an “equipment corridor”.

### Public interest as a spatial standard: four equivalent paths

Basic public-service nodes should provide **AI, human, offline, and low/no-sensing paths**. Equivalence concerns the basic service outcome, not identical interfaces: a wheelchair user should complete the same essential service; an older person should not need a commercial account; connectivity failure should not erase basic access; and responsible humans and complaint/appeal routes should remain visible. This turns AI ethics into a plan/section/node acceptance standard. [standard:BARRIER-FREE-ENVIRONMENT-LAW] [depth:blue_green_public_space]
